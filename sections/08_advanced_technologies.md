# 8. Advanced Solar Technologies

To surpass the S-Q limit and reduce costs, researchers are moving beyond traditional single-junction silicon.

### 8.1 Multi-Junction (Tandem) Cells

By stacking multiple semiconductors with different bandgaps, a cell can capture different parts of the solar spectrum more efficiently. High-energy photons are captured by the top layer (large $E_g$), while low-energy photons pass through to be captured by the bottom layer (small $E_g$). This significantly reduces thermalization losses. These cells have reached efficiencies exceeding **47%** under concentrated sunlight.

### 8.2 Perovskite Solar Cells

Perovskites are a class of materials with a specific crystal structure ($ABX_3$) that can be manufactured using cheap, low-temperature solution processing. Their bandgap is easily tunable, making them ideal candidates for the top layer in silicon-perovskite tandem cells, which are projected to reach 30% efficiency in commercial modules soon.

### 8.3 Bifacial Panels and Tracking

Bifacial panels capture light from both the front and the rear (reflected from the ground, or "albedo"). Combined with single-axis tracking systems that follow the sun's path, these technologies can increase energy yield by up to 30% without requiring new semiconductor physics.
