# 9. Energy Storage and Grid Integration

The intermittency of renewable sources requires advanced physical systems for energy storage and distribution.

### 9.1 Battery Physics: Li-ion and Solid-State

Lithium-ion batteries store energy via the reversible movement of $Li^+$ ions between an anode (usually graphite) and a cathode (transition metal oxide). The energy density is limited by the chemistry of these materials and the risk of "dendrite" formation in liquid electrolytes. **Solid-state batteries** replace the liquid with a solid ceramic or polymer, offering higher safety and significantly higher energy density by allowing the use of lithium metal anodes.

### 9.2 Hydrogen as an Energy Carrier

Surplus renewable electricity can power **electrolyzers** to split water:
$$ 2H_2O + \text{energy} \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2 $$
This hydrogen can be stored and later converted back to electricity via fuel cells or used as a clean fuel for heavy industry, effectively acting as a high-capacity "chemical battery."

### 9.3 Smart Grids and Intermittency

Grid integration is a challenge of balancing supply and demand in real-time. Smart grids use AI and advanced power electronics (Inverters based on Wide-Bandgap semiconductors like $SiC$ and $GaN$) to manage the variable input from wind and solar while maintaining the 50/60 Hz frequency stability required for industrial machinery.
