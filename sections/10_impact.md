# 10. Environmental and Economic Impact

The physics of renewable energy translates directly into environmental and economic metrics.

### 10.1 Lifecycle Analysis (LCA) and $CO_2$ Reduction

The "energy payback time" for a silicon solar panel is currently between 1 and 2 years. Given a lifespan of 25–30 years, the net carbon reduction is massive. Unlike fossil fuels, where the energy is released by breaking chemical bonds and releasing carbon, renewable systems leverage existing physical gradients (thermal, kinetic, or electromagnetic).

### 10.2 Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE)

LCOE represents the average revenue per unit of electricity generated that would be required to recover the costs of building and operating a generating plant. Due to the modular nature of solar and wind and the lack of fuel costs, their LCOE has plummeted—solar PV is now the cheapest source of new electricity in most of the world.

### 10.3 Land Use and Ecology

A common critique is the land footprint of solar and wind. However, "Agrivoltaics"—the simultaneous use of land for both solar power and agriculture—demonstrates that these systems can actually improve crop yields by providing shade and reducing water evaporation, a synergistic physical interaction.
